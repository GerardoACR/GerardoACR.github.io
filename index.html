About me

I've always enjoyed learning, asking questions, and exploring very different disciplines. I studied Philosophy in Mexico, followed by a master's degree in Governance and Digital Innovation in Italy. Today, I research artificial intelligence applied to climate science at the University of Bologna. To me, philosophy, science, and technological development are not separate worlds: they are different ways of understanding the world, acting within it, and questioning the consequences of our actions. I'm particularly interested in the ethical responsibility involved in developing new technologies, how they allow us to transform the world, intentionally or otherwise, and what those transformations mean for the different forms of life with which we coexist and the possible futures our decisions may open up or close off.

I'm interested in developing technologies that can be genuinely useful, whether in research, the private sector, public institutions, or local communities. My experience with weather models and AI agents has taught me that making predictions is not enough. We also need to ask how reliable our tools are, how we interpret their results, and what consequences may follow from the decisions we make based on them. I believe that before acting, we need to observe carefully, critically examine the evidence, and question our own interpretations. After all, thinking critically also means recognizing our limitations, considering different perspectives through dialogue, and remaining open to what we can learn from others.

Teaching holds a very special place in my life. I enjoy sharing what I learn, communicating science and philosophy, and talking with people whose perspectives differ from my own. In my contemporary philosophy classes, I encourage students to question what seems obvious, develop independent judgment, and understand that the technological future is not already written. Teaching is also one of the ways I continue to learn and question my own assumptions.

Outside research, I'm passionate about art, cinema, audiovisual creation, and video games. I love discovering stories, exploring imaginary worlds, and experimenting with different forms of expression.

Ultimately, I hope that my work and what I share with others can contribute, even in a small way, to expanding our possibilities for understanding and caring for the world we inhabit.

Master’s thesis · University of Bologna × Hypermeteo

Deep Learning for Solar Irradiance Nowcasting: An End-to-end Evaluation over Europe [ENG]

In collaboration with Hypermeteo, I developed and evaluated a ConvLSTM-based Deep Learning model for forecasting direct and global solar irradiance across Europe, with forecast horizons ranging from 30 minutes to six hours. I built an end-to-end computational pipeline covering data acquisition, cleaning, spatial and temporal harmonization, normalization, and integration of satellite, atmospheric, and geographical data, followed by distributed multi-GPU training using PyTorch Distributed Data Parallel (DDP) on HPC infrastructure with support from CINECA. The project also involved continental-scale inference and forecast reconstruction, along with a comprehensive scientific evaluation of the model's predictive accuracy, spatiotemporal behavior, and limitations. Additionally, I implemented an inference pathway using atmospheric data available at forecast time, laying the groundwork for future operational deployment.
